## How to use this manual

This manual describes how money moves through this firm and what the CFO is
responsible for at each step. It is written to be worked through in order the
first time and used as a reference afterwards.

Three conventions run through it:

**Procedures are numbered and are meant to be followed literally.** Where a
procedure says to obtain something in writing, a verbal answer does not close
the step.

**Rules are described by subject, never cited by number.** Rule numbering and
text change. Cite the rule itself, not this manual. Anything marked *Verify*
is a claim you must confirm with counsel before relying on it.

**Links are landing pages, not deep links.** They were not individually
verified when this manual was issued. If one fails, search the site rather
than assuming the resource is gone. Never quote a government fee, threshold,
or processing time from this manual — pull the live page.

### What this manual is not

It is not legal advice, and it does not resolve any question of professional
responsibility. Several sections identify questions that must be answered by
the firm's attorney or by outside ethics counsel. The CFO's role in those
sections is to produce the facts and escalate — not to decide.

## 1. Structure and authority

### 1.1 The single admission

Every dollar this firm bills traces back to one admission. José Rolando is
admitted in Puerto Rico. Everyone else at the firm works under that admission.

Nothing about that is unusual in immigration practice. It becomes consequential
in two places, and both belong to the CFO: the boundary of what the admission
authorizes (section 1.2), and the risk of the whole firm depending on it
(section 1.5).

### 1.2 The three authority buckets

Our work falls into three buckets. They are not the same, and the boundary does
not follow the office address.

| Bucket | Status | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Federal immigration** — USCIS, the immigration courts, the BIA | Authorized | Federal regulation defines an attorney as a member in good standing of the bar of any state, possession, territory, or commonwealth. Puerto Rico qualifies, and geography is irrelevant. See [8 CFR Part 1001](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-V/subchapter-A/part-1001) and [8 CFR Part 292](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-V/subchapter-B/part-292). |
| **2. Federal district court** — mandamus and APA suits against USCIS | Admission required | Each district admits its own bar, and Texas districts commonly tie admission to Texas bar membership. Verify per district, every district. |
| **3. Texas state court** and advice on Texas law | Not covered | A Puerto Rico admission does not reach Texas state courts. Requires Texas admission, pro hac vice for a named case, or a Texas-licensed attorney genuinely of record. |

*Verify:* bucket 2 against the local rules of each federal district we file in
— [S.D. Tex.](https://www.txs.uscourts.gov/), [N.D. Tex.](https://www.txnd.uscourts.gov/),
[W.D. Tex.](https://www.txwd.uscourts.gov/), [E.D. Tex.](https://www.txed.uscourts.gov/)
— and bucket 3 with Texas counsel. Do not run the firm off this table.

### 1.3 Procedure: the authority audit

Run this once in your first week, then quarterly.

1. Export every matter opened, worked, or closed in the last twelve months.
2. For each matter record: matter type, forum, the specific admission or
   authority relied on, revenue recognized in the period, and bucket.
3. Total revenue by bucket and express each as a percentage of the whole.
4. List separately every matter in bucket 2 or 3 with no named admission and
   no Texas attorney of record. Attach a dollar figure to that list.
5. Deliver the list to José Rolando and to outside ethics counsel the same
   week. Do not hold it for a monthly report.
6. Record the date delivered and the response received. File both.

**Output:** a revenue split across three buckets, and a named exceptions list
with a number against it.

**Boundary:** you produce the number and escalate it. The attorney and ethics
counsel decide what it means. Do not opine on whether a matter was properly
handled, and do not re-characterize a matter to make it fit a bucket.

### 1.4 Procedure: classifying a new matter

Applies at intake, before the engagement letter is signed.

1. Identify the forum the matter will be filed or heard in.
2. Identify the authority that permits this firm to appear there.
3. If the answer is not bucket 1, do not open the matter until either a named
   admission or a Texas attorney of record is recorded against it.
4. Record the bucket on the matter record. It is a reportable field, not a note.
5. If the matter cannot be classified, that is an escalation, not a judgment
   call. See section 1.6.

### 1.5 Concentration risk

One admission carries one hundred percent of the firm's billable authority. If
it stops — illness, suspension, a disciplinary complaint, retirement — the firm
cannot lawfully bill for work in progress. Not reduced. Stopped.

Treat this the way you would treat a single customer who is the entire order
book. The CFO owns the mitigations:

| Mitigation | What "done" looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Succession plan | Written, naming who takes the files and on what authority, approved before it is needed |
| Bench strength | A second admitted attorney, or standing Texas co-counsel under a signed retainer |
| Key-person cover | Disability and key-person cover sized to revenue actually at stake, reviewed annually |
| Malpractice cover | A policy that accurately describes the jurisdictions we practice in. Misdescribing scope is how firms discover an exclusion during a claim |
| Cash buffer | Enough operating reserve to wind down or transfer matters in an orderly way |

### 1.6 Escalation

| Trigger | Goes to | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A matter cannot be classified into a bucket | Attorney, then ethics counsel | Same day |
| Bucket 2 or 3 revenue with no cover | Attorney and ethics counsel | Same week, in writing |
| Any trust account discrepancy | Attorney, immediately | Immediately, before any further transfer |
| A disciplinary inquiry from any jurisdiction | Attorney and insurer | Same day |
| A client asks who is representing them | Attorney | Before answering |

## 2. Money held for clients

This is the section where an honest mistake ends careers. Read it before you
touch the account.

### 2.1 Which rules govern us

Two bodies of professional conduct rules can reach this firm at once.

| Jurisdiction | Why it reaches us |
| --- | --- |
| **Puerto Rico** | Where the admission lives. The Canons of Professional Ethics adopted by the Supreme Court of Puerto Rico govern the attorney, and the PR disciplinary authority can act on conduct anywhere. [Rama Judicial de Puerto Rico](https://poderjudicial.pr/) · [Colegio de Abogados y Notarios de PR](https://www.capr.org/) |
| **Texas** | Where the practice, the clients, the office, and the bank account are. Texas asserts disciplinary authority over lawyers who practice here whether or not they are admitted here. [Texas Center for Legal Ethics](https://www.legalethicstexas.com/) |

**The working rule until counsel says otherwise:** comply with whichever
requirement is stricter on every point, hold client funds in Texas at an
approved institution, and obtain a written choice-of-law opinion.

*Verify:* this is a genuine legal question, not an accounting preference. Get
the opinion in writing, keep it on file, and re-request it if the firm's
footprint changes. A documented decision you can produce on request is worth
more than being right by accident.

Texas IOLTA is administered by the
[Texas Access to Justice Foundation](https://www.teajf.org/). Confirm our trust
account is actually at an approved institution. Do not assume the bank handled
it when the account was opened.

### 2.2 The absolute rules

These do not bend for cash flow, for a good quarter, or for a client who said
it was fine.

- Client money is not the firm's money.
- Unearned fees stay in trust until earned.
- Firm money does not sit in trust, beyond any minimum the bank requires to
  keep the account open.
- No transfer out of trust without a documented earning event.
- No disbursement against uncollected funds.
- Trust is never overdrawn. There is no such thing as a small trust overdraft.
- Every client's balance is tracked individually, not just in aggregate.

Under a single-admission structure, a trust violation is not a fine and a
lesson. It is an extinction event for the firm.

### 2.3 Procedure: receiving client funds

1. Determine whether the funds are earned on receipt. In a flat-fee immigration
   practice they usually are not.
2. Deposit unearned funds into the trust account, never into operating.
3. Record the deposit against the individual client ledger the same day.
4. Where the payment covers a government filing fee, record that portion as
   funds held for a specific purpose, not as firm revenue. See section 3.2.
5. If a card processor nets its fee out of the deposit, stop. A processor that
   deducts fees from a trust deposit creates an ethics problem. Use legal-
   specific processing that debits fees from operating.

### 2.4 Procedure: earning a flat fee

Flat fees are the norm in immigration, and a flat fee paid up front is
generally still unearned. It belongs in trust until the work that earns it is
done.

1. Identify the earning milestones in the signed fee agreement. If you cannot
   find them, escalate — see the note below.
2. Confirm the milestone has actually occurred, from the case management
   system, not from a conversation.
3. Prepare a transfer for the amount earned at that milestone, and no more.
4. Record the earning event against the client ledger with the date, the
   milestone, and the evidence relied on.
5. Transfer trust to operating. Never the reverse.
6. Recognize revenue in the period the milestone occurred.

**If the milestones are vague or absent:** that is the first thing to fix, and
it is a matter for the attorney, not the CFO acting alone. A fee agreement the
client cannot read the milestones out of is a problem regardless of what the
firm intended.

### 2.5 Procedure: monthly three-way reconciliation

Run this every month, without exception, and do the first one by hand.

1. Reconcile the trust bank statement to the trust general ledger.
2. Reconcile the trust general ledger to the sum of all individual client
   ledgers.
3. Confirm all three agree to the cent. They must agree, not approximately
   agree.
4. Investigate any difference before doing anything else. Do not net, do not
   carry forward, do not adjust to make it balance.
5. Confirm no individual client ledger has a negative balance. A negative
   client balance means one client's funds were used for another's matter.
6. Have the reconciliation reviewed and signed by the attorney.
7. File it. Retain per the applicable record-keeping requirement.

*Verify:* record retention periods differ between jurisdictions. Confirm which
period applies to us and apply the longer one.

### 2.6 Red flags

Any of these means stop and escalate the same day:

- The three-way reconciliation does not tie.
- A client ledger is negative.
- A transfer out of trust has no documented earning event behind it.
- Trust funds were used to cover an operating shortfall, even briefly, even
  repaid.
- A filing fee was collected but the filing was never made.
- Someone asks you to delay a reconciliation.

## 3. The product: immigration filings

Bucket one is most of our book. A case there is a sequence of federal filings,
each carrying a fee, a clock, and a billing trigger.

### 3.1 What a case is

Understanding this is a prerequisite to forecasting anything:

- **A fee.** The government's cut is a pass-through cost, not revenue.
- **A clock.** Processing time decides when the next milestone can be billed
  and when cash actually lands. It is the firm's working-capital cycle.
- **A trigger.** Each milestone is the moment a slice of the flat fee stops
  being the client's money and becomes ours.

### 3.2 Accounting treatment of filing fees

A government filing fee collected from a client is not revenue and never was.
Booking it as revenue inflates the top line and hides the real margin.

1. Collect the filing fee as funds held for a specific purpose.
2. Hold it in trust unless and until it is paid to the government.
3. Pay it to the government and record the disbursement against the client
   ledger.
4. Never recognize it as revenue at any point in that sequence.
5. Reconcile collected-but-unpaid filing fees monthly. A fee collected and not
   filed is both a client problem and a trust problem.

### 3.3 Cohort risk: the Visa Bulletin

When a category in the [Visa Bulletin](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/legal/visa-law0/visa-bulletin.html)
retrogresses, every case behind that priority date stalls — and so does the
revenue attached to it. This is a revenue risk the firm does not control and
cannot influence.

1. Track our active caseload by category and priority date.
2. Model what a retrogression in each significant category does to the next
   two quarters of milestone billing.
3. Review at each monthly close.

## 4. Pricing and profitability

### 4.1 The five metrics

Law firms are measured differently from other businesses. Learn the first four
by name; the fifth is the one that matters most here.

| Metric | Formula | Question it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Utilization | billable hours ÷ available hours | Are we busy? |
| Realization | amount billed ÷ amount worked | What did we give away? |
| Collection | amount collected ÷ amount billed | What did we never get paid? |
| Client acquisition cost | marketing spend ÷ new signed matters | What does a client cost to win? |
| **Effective hourly rate** | **flat fee ÷ hours actually spent** | **Which work is actually worth doing?** |

Benchmarks for the first four are in the
[Clio Legal Trends Report](https://www.clio.com/resources/legal-trends/) — free,
annual, and the best source available for a firm our size. Broader context:
[Thomson Reuters Institute](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/institute) ·
[Georgetown Center on Ethics and the Legal Profession](https://www.law.georgetown.edu/legal-profession-center/).

### 4.2 Procedure: effective hourly rate by case type

Run this quarterly.

1. Select a case type from the matter classification.
2. Pull every matter of that type closed in the last twelve months.
3. Sum all recorded hours against those matters, from every timekeeper.
4. Sum the flat fees actually earned, excluding pass-through filing fees.
5. Divide fees by hours. That is the effective hourly rate for the case type.
6. Compare against our standard hourly rate and against other case types.
7. Weight the result by authority bucket. A strong effective hourly rate in
   bucket 3 is an exposure, not a growth opportunity.

**Expect the hours to be incomplete.** Flat-fee matters are chronically
under-recorded because nobody bills against them. If time capture on flat-fee
work is poor, fixing it is a prerequisite to trusting this metric, and it
belongs on the process backlog.

### 4.3 Procedure: quarterly case-mix review

1. Refresh the effective hourly rate for every case type.
2. Refresh the authority audit (section 1.3).
3. Identify one case type to do more of and one to stop taking.
4. Present both to the attorney with the arithmetic behind them.
5. Record the decision, including a decision to change nothing.

With one attorney carrying every matter, those hours are the scarcest thing
this firm owns. Choosing the wrong case mix is a capacity problem before it is
a margin problem.

### 5.3 Open question: Puerto Rico

An admission in Puerto Rico is not by itself a tax nexus. An entity, an office,
or resident work there would change the picture.

1. Put the question to the firm's CPA in writing in your first week.
2. Obtain the answer in writing.
3. File it with the choice-of-law opinion from section 2.1.
4. Re-ask if the firm's footprint changes.

### 5.4 Employment traps

most expensive payroll mistake law firms make. Review how our paralegals are
classified and document the basis.

verification is both a bad look and a real liability.

## 6. Systems and controls

### 6.1 The system-of-record map

Document which system is authoritative for each of the following. Where two
systems both claim one, that is a finding.

- Matters and matter status
- Time recorded
- Invoices issued
- Cash received and held
- Client trust balances

Immigration case management: [Docketwise](https://www.docketwise.com/) ·
[INSZoom](https://www.inszoom.com/) · [Cerenade](https://www.cerenade.com/) ·
[LollyLaw](https://www.lollylaw.com/)

Practice management: [Clio](https://www.clio.com/) · [MyCase](https://www.mycase.com/)

Payments and accounting: [LawPay](https://www.lawpay.com/) ·
[QuickBooks Online](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/), with a chart of accounts
that separates trust from operating.

Every place a person retypes a number from one system into another is a place
our numbers will eventually disagree. Enumerate those points; that list is the
first process backlog.

### 6.2 Procedure: monthly close

1. Complete the three-way trust reconciliation (section 2.5) before anything
   else. Nothing else in the close is meaningful if trust does not tie.
2. Reconcile the operating account.
3. Confirm every trust-to-operating transfer in the period has a documented
   earning event.
4. Reconcile collected-but-unpaid filing fees.
5. Recognize revenue by milestone, not by cash receipt.
6. Update the rolling 13-week cash flow forecast.
7. Update the metrics dashboard.
8. Review the aged receivable and the aged WIP.
9. Close the period and file the pack, including the signed reconciliation.

### 6.3 Minimum internal controls

For a firm this size, segregation of duties is imperfect by necessity. These
are the minimum compensating controls:

- The person who reconciles the trust account is not the only person who can
  move money out of it.
- Every trust disbursement has a second pair of eyes before release.
- The attorney reviews and signs the monthly trust reconciliation.
- Bank statements are received by someone other than the person who reconciles.
- No shared logins for banking or payment processing.

## 7. The first 90 days

### 7.1 Days 1–30: establish the ground

- [ ] Read section 2 of this manual end to end before touching the account
- [ ] Run the authority audit (section 1.3) and deliver the exceptions list
- [ ] Request the written choice-of-law opinion
- [ ] Reconcile the trust account yourself, by hand, once
- [ ] Confirm the trust account is at an approved institution
- [ ] Read the standard fee agreement closely and locate the earning milestones
- [ ] Put the Puerto Rico tax question to the CPA in writing
- [ ] Sit in on three client consultations

### 7.2 Days 31–60: learn the product

- [ ] Build the case-type matrix: government fee, flat fee, cycle time, bucket
- [ ] Compute effective hourly rate across twelve months of closed matters
- [ ] Assess time capture on flat-fee work and fix it if it is unreliable
- [ ] Map the systems of record and list every re-keying point
- [ ] Review paralegal exempt/non-exempt classification
- [ ] Read the current Clio Legal Trends Report and mark where we are off

### 7.3 Days 61–90: build the instruments

- [ ] A rolling 13-week cash flow forecast
- [ ] A monthly close calendar covering federal and Texas obligations
- [ ] A metrics dashboard the attorney will actually open
- [ ] A succession and key-person plan with a number attached
- [ ] One case-mix recommendation, weighted by authority bucket
- [ ] Confirm malpractice cover accurately describes our jurisdictions
