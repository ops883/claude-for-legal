# Becoming a CFO of an Immigration Law Firm — Resource Links

A starter reading list for a new CFO. The order matters: sections 1–3 are the
things that will bite you in month one, sections 4–7 are the things that make
you good at the job by month six.

> **Note on links:** these are landing pages chosen because they tend to stay
> put, rather than deep links that rot. Government fee and processing-time
> pages change often — always pull the live page, never a cached number.
> Nobody re-verified every URL in this file when it was written; if one 404s,
> search the site rather than assuming the resource is gone.

## 1. Know the product you're financing: immigration filings

You cannot forecast revenue in this firm without knowing what a case *is*: a
sequence of government filings, each with its own government fee, its own
processing time, and its own trigger for billing the client.

| Resource | Why the CFO cares |
| --- | --- |
| [USCIS filing fees](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/filing-fees) | Every filing fee, by form. These are pass-through costs, not revenue — get this distinction right on day one. |
| [USCIS Form G-1055 (fee schedule)](https://www.uscis.gov/g-1055) | The consolidated fee schedule in one PDF. Good for building a fee lookup table. |
| [All USCIS forms](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/all-forms) | The catalog of what the firm actually files (I-130, I-485, I-589, I-751, N-400, I-765…). |
| [USCIS processing times](https://egov.uscis.gov/processing-times/) | Drives cash timing on milestone billing and your work-in-progress estimates. |
| [USCIS Policy Manual](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual) | Not light reading, but skim the table of contents so you understand case categories. |
| [Department of State — visa fees](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/fees/fees-visa-services.html) | Consular fees for cases that finish abroad. |
| [Visa Bulletin](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/legal/visa-law0/visa-bulletin.html) | Priority-date movement. When a category retrogresses, a whole cohort of cases stalls — and so does the revenue attached to them. |
| [EOIR (immigration courts)](https://www.justice.gov/eoir) | Removal-defense docket. Court cases bill very differently from affirmative filings. |
| [DOL Office of Foreign Labor Certification](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/foreign-labor) and [FLAG system](https://flag.dol.gov/) | PERM / LCA work if the firm does employment-based cases. |
| [Federal Register — USCIS](https://www.federalregister.gov/agencies/u-s-citizenship-and-immigration-services) | Where fee increases and rule changes are announced *before* they hit. Set an alert. |
| [AILA](https://www.aila.org/) | The trade association. Practice advisories, and the place the attorneys already live. |

**Action item:** build a one-page matrix of the firm's top 10 case types →
government fee, typical flat fee charged, typical months to completion. That
document is the foundation of every forecast you will ever build here.

## 2. Trust accounting and legal ethics — the part that can end the firm

This is the single biggest difference between a law firm CFO and any other
CFO. Client money is not the firm's money. Commingling it, or borrowing from
it, is a disbarment-level offense for the attorneys and a career-ending one
for you.

- [ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/)
  — read **Rule 1.15 (Safekeeping Property)** and **Rule 1.5 (Fees)** in full.
  Everything else can wait; these two cannot.
- [ABA IOLTA resources](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/interest_lawyers_trust_accounts/)
  — how interest on client trust accounts works and where it goes.
- **Your state bar's trust accounting rules** — the Model Rules are a model;
  your state's version is the one that governs. Find your state's page via the
  [ABA state bar directory](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/legal_services/flh-home/flh-bar-directories-and-lawyer-finders/).
  *Fill in the direct link for our state and keep it at the top of this list.*
- [ABA Law Practice Division](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/law_practice/)
  — law firm management generally, including finance and technology.

**The immigration-specific wrinkle:** flat fees are the norm in immigration,
and a flat fee paid up front is generally still *unearned* — it belongs in
trust until the work that earns it is done. Know exactly what your state
requires for flat fees, milestone earning, and the disclosures that go in the
retainer agreement. Get the managing attorney to walk you through the firm's
current fee agreement line by line.

## 3. Small-business finance, tax, and payroll

- [IRS — Small Business and Self-Employed](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed)
- [IRS Publication 334 — Tax Guide for Small Business](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p334)
- [IRS Publication 583 — Starting a Business and Keeping Records](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p583)
- [IRS forms and publications](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs) — you will live
  in 941, 940, W-2/W-3, 1099-NEC, and whichever entity return applies
  (1120-S or 1065).
- [EFTPS](https://www.eftps.gov/) — federal tax deposits.
- [DOL Wage and Hour Division](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd) — overtime and
  classification. Paralegal exempt/non-exempt classification is a common and
  expensive mistake in law firms.
- [I-9 Central](https://www.uscis.gov/i-9-central) and
  [E-Verify](https://www.e-verify.gov/) — an immigration firm being sloppy
  about its *own* I-9s is a bad look and a real liability.
- [SBA](https://www.sba.gov/) — financing, and general small-business guidance.
- [AICPA & CIMA](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/) and [FASB](https://www.fasb.org/)
  — revenue recognition (ASC 606) is the standard that tells you when a flat
  fee becomes revenue. Read it against the trust accounting rules in §2; they
  answer different questions and both must be satisfied.

## 4. Law firm economics and benchmarks

- [Clio Legal Trends Report](https://www.clio.com/resources/legal-trends/) —
  free, annual, and the best single source for small-firm benchmarks:
  utilization rate, realization rate, collection rate, cost per acquired
  client. Start here.
- [Thomson Reuters Institute](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/institute)
  — legal market reports, skewed larger-firm but useful for direction of travel.
- [Georgetown Law — Center on Ethics and the Legal Profession](https://www.law.georgetown.edu/legal-profession-center/)
  — publishes the annual *Report on the State of the Legal Market* with
  Thomson Reuters.

**The four metrics to learn first**, because law firms are measured
differently than other businesses:

1. **Utilization** — billable hours worked ÷ available hours.
2. **Realization** — amount billed ÷ amount worked (what you gave away).
3. **Collection** — amount collected ÷ amount billed (what you never got paid).
4. **Cost per acquired client (CAC)** — marketing spend ÷ new signed matters.

For a flat-fee immigration practice, add: **effective hourly rate per case
type** (flat fee ÷ hours actually spent). That number is where the firm's
profitability is really decided, and it is usually nobody's job to track it
until a CFO shows up.

## 5. Systems the firm runs on

Immigration-specific practice management (case management + forms automation):

- [Docketwise](https://www.docketwise.com/)
- [INSZoom](https://www.inszoom.com/)
- [Cerenade](https://www.cerenade.com/)
- [LollyLaw](https://www.lollylaw.com/)

General practice management and billing:

- [Clio](https://www.clio.com/)
- [MyCase](https://www.mycase.com/)

Payments and accounting:

- [LawPay](https://www.lawpay.com/) — legal-specific payment processing that
  keeps trust and operating funds separated correctly. Generic processors that
  net out fees from a trust deposit create an ethics problem.
- [QuickBooks Online](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/) — with a chart of
  accounts set up for trust vs. operating.

**Action item:** map the current stack — what system is the system of record
for matters, for time, for invoices, for cash? Where does data get re-keyed by
hand? That map is your first process-improvement backlog.

## 7. A 90-day path through all of the above

**Days 1–30 — Don't break anything.**
Read §2 end to end, including our state's trust accounting rules. Reconcile
the trust account yourself, by hand, once. Sit with the intake team and watch
three consultations. Read the standard fee agreement.

**Days 31–60 — Learn the product.**
Build the case-type matrix from §1 (fee, government cost, cycle time). Pull
twelve months of closed matters and calculate effective hourly rate by case
type. Read the current Clio Legal Trends Report and mark where we're off
benchmark.

**Days 61–90 — Build the instruments.**
A rolling 13-week cash flow forecast. A monthly close calendar. A dashboard of
the metrics in §4 that the managing attorney will actually look at. Then bring
one recommendation: the case type we should do more of, and the one we should
stop taking.
