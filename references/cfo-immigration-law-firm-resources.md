# Becoming Our CFO — Resource Links

A starter reading list for an incoming CFO of this firm specifically: a Texas
practice operating on a Puerto Rico license held by José Rolando, with
everyone else working under that admission.

The order matters. Section 1 is the structural fact everything else depends
on. Sections 2–4 are the things that will bite in month one. Sections 5–8 are
the things that make you good at the job by month six.

> **Note on links:** these are landing pages chosen because they tend to stay
> put, rather than deep links that rot. Nobody re-verified every URL when this
> was written; if one 404s, search the site rather than assuming the resource
> is gone. Government fee, tax and processing-time pages change often — always
> pull the live page, never a cached number.

> **Note on rules:** where this document describes a rule, it describes it by
> subject rather than by number. Cite the rule, never this file. Rule numbering
> and text change, and a wrong citation is worse than none.

## 1. Authority: what our license actually covers

Every dollar this firm bills traces back to one admission. Before forecasting
anything, know what it does and does not authorize. Our work falls into three
buckets, and they are not the same.

| Bucket | Status | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Federal immigration** — USCIS, the immigration courts, the BIA | Authorized | Federal regulation defines an attorney as a member in good standing of the bar of any state, possession, territory, or commonwealth. Puerto Rico qualifies, and geography is irrelevant — the admission works the same in Houston as in San Juan. See [8 CFR Part 1001](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-V/subchapter-A/part-1001) (definition of "attorney") and [8 CFR Part 292](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-V/subchapter-B/part-292) (representation). |
| **Federal district court** — mandamus and APA suits against USCIS | Admission required | Each district admits its own bar, and Texas districts commonly tie that admission to Texas bar membership. Check the local rules of every district we file in, district by district: [S.D. Tex.](https://www.txs.uscourts.gov/), [N.D. Tex.](https://www.txnd.uscourts.gov/), [W.D. Tex.](https://www.txwd.uscourts.gov/), [E.D. Tex.](https://www.txed.uscourts.gov/). |
| **Texas state court** and advice on Texas law | Not covered | A Puerto Rico admission does not reach Texas state courts. That work needs Texas admission, pro hac vice for a named case, or a Texas-licensed attorney genuinely of record. [State Bar of Texas](https://www.texasbar.com/) · [Texas Judicial Branch](https://www.txcourts.gov/) |

**Action item, week one:** sort twelve months of matters into those three
buckets and put a revenue number against each. Anything in bucket 2 or 3
without a named admission or a Texas attorney of record behind it goes on one
list, with a dollar figure, and that list goes to José Rolando and outside
ethics counsel immediately — not at the end of your first ninety days.

**This is a legal question, not an accounting one.** Your job is to produce the
number and escalate it. The attorney and ethics counsel decide what it means.

### Concentration risk

One admission carries one hundred percent of the firm's billable authority. If
it stops — illness, suspension, a disciplinary complaint, retirement — the firm
cannot lawfully bill for work in progress. Not reduced; stopped. Treat it the
way you would treat a customer that is the entire order book:

- A written succession plan, approved before it is needed.
- A second admitted attorney, or standing Texas co-counsel on retainer.
- Disability and key-person cover sized to the revenue actually at stake.
- Malpractice cover that accurately describes the jurisdictions we practice in.
  Misdescribing scope is a good way to discover an exclusion during a claim.

## 2. Trust accounting: which rulebook governs us

Two bodies of professional conduct rules can reach this firm at once, and you
need to know which one you are complying with before you touch the account.

| | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- |
| **Puerto Rico** | Where the admission lives. The Canons of Professional Ethics adopted by the Supreme Court of Puerto Rico govern the attorney, and the PR disciplinary authority can act on conduct anywhere. [Rama Judicial de Puerto Rico](https://poderjudicial.pr/) · [Colegio de Abogados y Notarios de PR](https://www.capr.org/) |
| **Texas** | Where the practice, the clients, the office and the bank account are. Texas asserts disciplinary authority over lawyers who practice here whether or not they are admitted here. [Texas Center for Legal Ethics](https://www.legalethicstexas.com/) hosts the Texas Disciplinary Rules of Professional Conduct. |

**The working answer until counsel says otherwise:** comply with whichever rule
is stricter on every point, hold client funds in Texas at an approved
institution, and get the choice-of-law question answered *in writing*. A
documented decision you can produce on request is worth more than being right
by accident.

- [Texas Access to Justice Foundation](https://www.teajf.org/) — administers
  Texas IOLTA. Confirm our trust account is actually at an approved
  institution; do not assume the bank handled it when the account was opened.
- [ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/)
  — useful as the common vocabulary both jurisdictions draw on. Read the
  safekeeping-of-property and fees rules. Not binding on us by itself.

### The line

Client money is not the firm's money. Commingling it — or borrowing from it,
even briefly, even meaning to put it back — is a disbarment-level offense for
the attorney and a career-ending one for you. Under a single-admission
structure it is also an extinction event for the firm.

### The flat-fee wrinkle

Flat fees are the norm in immigration, and a flat fee paid up front is
generally still *unearned*. It belongs in trust until the work that earns it is
done. Find the earning milestones in our actual fee agreement. If you cannot
find them, neither can the client, and that is the first thing to fix.

## 3. The product: immigration filings

Bucket one is most of our book. A case there is a sequence of federal filings,
each with a fee, a clock, and a billing trigger.

| Resource | Why the CFO cares |
| --- | --- |
| [USCIS filing fees](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/filing-fees) and [Form G-1055](https://www.uscis.gov/g-1055) | Every filing fee, by form. Pass-through costs, not revenue — get this right in the chart of accounts on day one. |
| [All USCIS forms](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/all-forms) | The catalog of what we actually file (I-130, I-485, I-589, I-751, N-400, I-765…). |
| [USCIS processing times](https://egov.uscis.gov/processing-times/) | Your work-in-progress clock. A fourteen-month adjudication is fourteen months of WIP. |
| [Visa Bulletin](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/legal/visa-law0/visa-bulletin.html) | When a category retrogresses, every case behind that priority date stalls — and so does the revenue attached to it. |
| [Department of State — visa fees](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/fees/fees-visa-services.html) | Consular fees for cases that finish abroad. |
| [EOIR](https://www.justice.gov/eoir) and [EOIR forms](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/eoir-forms) | Removal defense. Court matters bill very differently from affirmative filings. |
| [Form G-28](https://www.uscis.gov/g-28) | The notice of appearance. Worth understanding what signing one commits the firm to. |
| [DOL Office of Foreign Labor Certification](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/foreign-labor) · [FLAG](https://flag.dol.gov/) | PERM and LCA work, if we take employment-based cases. |
| [Federal Register — USCIS](https://www.federalregister.gov/agencies/u-s-citizenship-and-immigration-services) | Where fee increases and rule changes appear *before* they hit. Set an alert. |
| [AILA](https://www.aila.org/) | Practice advisories, and where the immigration bar lives. |

## 4. Texas tax, payroll and compliance

| Federal | Texas |
| --- | --- |
| [EFTPS](https://www.eftps.gov/) deposits on the schedule the IRS assigns us | No state income tax — but the [franchise (margin) tax](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/franchise/) report is still due annually, whether or not tax is owed |
| Form 941 each quarter | [Texas Workforce Commission](https://www.twc.texas.gov/) unemployment tax, quarterly |
| 940, W-2/W-3 and 1099-NEC annually | Verify the current no-tax-due threshold each year rather than assuming last year's |
| The entity return — 1120-S or 1065 | [Texas Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/) for everything else |

Background reading: [IRS Small Business and Self-Employed](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed) ·
[Publication 334](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p334) ·
[Publication 583](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p583) ·
[IRS forms and publications](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs)

**Open question for the CPA, week one:** does Puerto Rico reach us for tax
purposes? An admission there is not by itself a nexus, but an entity, an
office, or resident work would change the picture. Put it in writing, get the
answer in writing, and file it.

**Two traps:**

- **Paralegal classification.** Exempt versus non-exempt is the most common and
  most expensive payroll mistake law firms make.
  [DOL Wage and Hour Division](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd)
- **Our own I-9s.** An immigration firm being sloppy about its own employment
  verification is both a bad look and a real liability.
  [I-9 Central](https://www.uscis.gov/i-9-central) · [E-Verify](https://www.e-verify.gov/)

## 5. Law firm economics

- [Clio Legal Trends Report](https://www.clio.com/resources/legal-trends/) —
  free, annual, and the best benchmark source for a firm our size.
- [Thomson Reuters Institute](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/institute) —
  skewed larger-firm, useful for direction of travel.
- [Georgetown Law — Center on Ethics and the Legal Profession](https://www.law.georgetown.edu/legal-profession-center/)
  — publishes the annual *Report on the State of the Legal Market*.

**The four metrics to learn first**, because law firms are measured differently
than other businesses:

1. **Utilization** — billable hours worked ÷ available hours.
2. **Realization** — amount billed ÷ amount worked (what we gave away).
3. **Collection** — amount collected ÷ amount billed (what we never got paid).
4. **Client acquisition cost** — marketing spend ÷ new signed matters.

**And the fifth, which is ours:** **effective hourly rate per case type** —
flat fee ÷ hours actually spent. Two case types can carry the same fee and
differ threefold in hours consumed. With one attorney carrying every matter,
those hours are the scarcest thing this firm owns, so choosing the wrong case
mix is a capacity problem before it is a margin problem. Weight the answer by
which authority bucket the work sits in: a strong effective hourly rate in
bucket 3 is an exposure, not a growth opportunity.

**Action item:** map what is the system of record for matters, for time, for
invoices, and for cash. Every place a person retypes a number from one system
into another is a place our numbers will eventually disagree. That list is your
first process backlog.

## 8. The first 90 days

**Days 1–30 — Establish the ground.**
Sort revenue into the three authority buckets and close that list out with
José Rolando and ethics counsel. Get the choice-of-law question answered in
writing. Reconcile the trust account yourself, by hand, once. Read the standard
fee agreement closely. Sit in on three consultations.

**Days 31–60 — Learn the product.**
Build the case-type matrix: government fee, flat fee, cycle time, authority
bucket. Compute effective hourly rate across twelve months of closed matters.
Confirm the trust account sits at an approved institution. Put the Puerto Rico
tax question to the CPA. Map where staff re-key numbers between systems.

**Days 61–90 — Build the instruments.**
A rolling 13-week cash flow forecast. A monthly close calendar covering both
federal and Texas filings. A succession and key-person plan with a number
attached. One recommendation on case mix, weighted by bucket.
